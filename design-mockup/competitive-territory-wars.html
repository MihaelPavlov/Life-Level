Competitive Engagement Concept

Conquest — the map itself becomes the leaderboard

Life-Level's zones already track real distance. Conquest makes that shared world contestable: guilds fight to hold territory, and every player — guild or not — gets a low-commitment rival to push against. Neither exists in Strava's flat kudos feed or Zwift's one-off races.

Why not just leaderboards
Global leaderboards are demotivating past top 1% and what Strava plateau users already have kudos-fatigue on. Conquest ranks territory and rivals, not raw totals.
Reuses what's built
Sieges reuse WorldZone + the Boss damage formula + the planned Guild module. Rivals reuses rank brackets from the Title/Rank ladder. No new game math.
Fixes the cold-start risk
Marketing flagged guilds as a promise we can't keep without critical mass. Rivals need only 2 players and work from day one — Sieges layer on once guilds exist.

Zone SiegesThe world map, now a contested board

Every zone already exists. Conquest adds ownership: a guild controls a zone by out-training every rival guild's cumulative effort there over a rolling 7-day window. Ownership is visible to everyone traveling through.

RivalsNo guild? You still get an opponent

Every player is auto-matched weekly against one opponent in the same rank bracket. One rotating stat category, a head-to-head bar, no leaderboard scroll to get lost in. This is the part that works with zero critical mass.

SeasonalHall of Legends — where the bragging happens

A season runs 6 weeks and ends on Reclamation Day, when every zone resets to unclaimed and the map redraws. Guild banners and rival streaks are both scored here — pairs with the already-designed Seasonal Events reward ladder.
